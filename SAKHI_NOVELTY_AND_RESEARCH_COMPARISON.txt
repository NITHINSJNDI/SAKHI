1. SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Women's health tools in India are split up. Period trackers, pregnancy apps,
government programmes (Kilkari, ANMOL, PMSMA) and menstrual hygiene campaigns
each cover one piece. Most consumer apps are built for English-speaking,
single-phone, always-online users.

Sakhi puts menstrual health first and follows the same woman from her
periods into pregnancy and after birth. It is built for Indian conditions:
Indian languages and voice, shared phones, patchy networks, government schemes
and ASHA workers. It is careful about safety: a rule-based safety check runs
before any AI, and risk scores only use readings she actually entered.


2. WHAT IS NEW, BY TYPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. TECHNICAL INNOVATION

[1] Safety check before any AI
    Every chat message goes through a rule-based red-flag check
    (lib/chat/safety.ts) before Gemini or any other model sees it. Danger signs
    get the same urgent guidance every time: heavy bleeding, severe headache
    with vision changes, reduced fetal movement, waters breaking, self-harm
    language. A model can never talk her out of going to hospital.
    Covered by automated tests (frontend/tests/safety.test.ts).

[2] Keeps working without AI, the ML service or a connection
    - No Gemini key: the offline chat engine answers common questions and
      still logs vitals, symptoms and tablets.
    - No Python service: the TypeScript copy of the same risk models runs
      instead. A parity script checks the two copies agree.
    - No signal: logs wait on the phone and are sent when the connection
      returns.
    Few consumer health apps are designed to keep working when each of these
    is missing.

[3] No made-up readings
    Risk models score only values she entered. A missing input is shown as
    "not scored"; it is never filled with a default value.

[4] Explainable screening
    - Preeclampsia and gestational diabetes use logistic regression trained on
      public datasets (5-fold ROC-AUC 0.84 and 0.83). Each result shows its
      main contributing factors and the clinical rules that fired.
    - PCOS, thyroid and cycle screens are RULE-BASED (Rotterdam-style
      patterns, symptom screens, cycle statistics), not machine learning.
      None of these is a diagnosis.

[5] Lab report reading with a review step
    She photographs a lab report and Sakhi reads Hb, sugars, HbA1c, TSH,
    ferritin and other values. Gemini vision reads it, with on-device OCR
    (tesseract.js) as a fallback. Every value is shown to her before saving and
    checked against WHO, IADPSG/DIPSI and ATA ranges.

[6] Cycle engine that learns her own cycle
    Uses her own cycle and period lengths, keeps a long bleed as one period,
    handles late and very late periods, and shows a confidence level instead
    of assuming 28 days. This is in line with Bull et al. (2019), which found
    that most cycles are not 28 days long.

B. MENSTRUAL HEALTH AND HYGIENE

[7] Period care module (new)
    Built into Her Mode (frontend/lib/hygiene.ts, components/her/HygieneCard.tsx):
    - Guidance for pads, cloth, menstrual cups, tampons and period underwear:
      how to use each safely, including washing and sun-drying cloth.
    - A change timer that reminds her when a change is due (6 hours for pads
      and cloth, 8 for tampons, 12 for cups) and warns if she is changing
      very often.
    - A supply check: estimates how many pads her next period needs from her
      own period length and flow, and warns before she runs out.
    - Safe disposal advice for each product.
    - Low-cost access: Jan Aushadhi Suvidha pads and free supplies through
      ASHA workers and health centres, with a map search for the nearest one.
    - Warning signs of infection and toxic shock.
    - A daily myth-busting card (bathing, food, "impurity", exercise).
    - The offline chat answers hygiene questions without AI.

[8] Anaemia watch linked to periods
    Combines heavy-flow days, long periods, tiredness, dizziness and Hb or
    ferritin results into a clear / watch / check signal, with iron-rich
    Indian foods.

C. SOCIAL INNOVATION

[9] Designed for shared phones
    Discreet mode: PIN lock, a neutral tab title and notifications, and a
    quick-hide button.

[10] Links to the public health system
    Checks eligibility for government schemes (PMMVY, JSY, JSSK, PMSMA).
    Includes a vaccine schedule on India's Universal Immunisation Programme,
    helpline numbers (108, 102, 104, 181, Tele-MANAS 14416), and map search
    for government hospitals, PHCs and Jan Aushadhi Kendras.

[11] Family alert
    Sends a ready-written WhatsApp or SMS to up to three contacts when a red
    flag appears, with her location only if she chooses to include it.

D. INDIA-SPECIFIC DESIGN

[12] Tablet ledger the Indian way
    Morning, afternoon, evening and night slots, before or after food, and
    1-0-1 patterns. Due times are worked out from her own meal times.

[13] Languages and voice
    Replies in English, Hindi, Tamil and Telugu. Voice in and out through the
    Web Speech API. An animated avatar's mouth moves while Sakhi speaks.

[14] One record from periods to pregnancy to after birth
    One account and one data store from periods, through pregnancy, to after
    birth: she does not need a new app at each stage. (The pregnancy risk
    models do not yet use her cycle history as an input. That is on the
    roadmap.)

4. HONEST LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Not clinically validated on Indian women. The risk models are trained on
  small public datasets (UCI Maternal Health Risk, 452 unique rows; Pima Indians
  Diabetes, 768 rows).
- No user pilot yet. All evidence so far comes from development and automated
  tests.
- Needs a smartphone and a browser. Women without their own phone are reached
  only through a shared phone or a future ASHA-assisted mode.
- Data is protected by per-user isolation, row-level security in PostgreSQL,
  httpOnly cookies and consent controls. Data at rest is not separately
  encrypted at the application level.


5. RELATED RESEARCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bull, J. R. et al. (2019). Real-world menstrual cycle characteristics of more
  than 600,000 menstrual cycles. npj Digital Medicine, 2, 83.
  -> Supports learning each woman's own cycle instead of assuming 28 days.
- Poon, L. C. et al. (2019). The FIGO initiative on pre-eclampsia: a pragmatic
  guide for first-trimester screening and prevention. Int J Gynecol Obstet,
  145(S1).
  -> The hospital standard uses Doppler and PlGF tests, which aren't
  available at home. Sakhi only does home blood-pressure monitoring and
  escalation; it does not replace FIGO screening.
- Marić, I. et al. (2020). Early prediction of preeclampsia via machine
  learning. AJOG MFM, 2(2), 100100.
  -> Blood pressure is a strong signal; Sakhi brings BP threshold alerts home.
- Artzi, N. S. et al. (2020). Prediction of gestational diabetes based on
  nationwide electronic health records. Nature Medicine, 26, 71-76.
  -> Uses hospital-record data; Sakhi uses sugar readings she logs herself.
- Miner, A. S. et al. (2016). Smartphone-based conversational agents and
  responses to questions about mental health, interpersonal violence, and
  physical health. JAMA Internal Medicine, 176(5), 619-625.
  -> General voice assistants respond poorly to health crises; Sakhi's safety
  check gives a fixed, tested response.
- PCOS machine-learning studies (for example Denny et al., 2019, ICSSIT) use
  hospital blood tests and ultrasound. Sakhi's PCOS screen is a rule-based
  pattern check on self-reported cycles and symptoms. [VERIFY exact citations]
