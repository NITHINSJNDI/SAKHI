## 2. Competitors in India, and why SAKHI is needed

### Who is already in the space

| Player | What it does | Reach and funding | Gap SAKHI fills |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kilkari** (MoHFW with ARMMAN) | Free weekly recorded voice calls on pregnancy and baby care | Reached **53 million** women; **4.3 million active**; 11 languages ([ARMMAN](https://armman.org/kilkari/)) | One-way messages only: no tracking, risk screening, period care or chat. **Not in Tamil.** |
| **ARMMAN mMitra** | Voice messages for urban pregnant women | Part of ARMMAN's programmes ([ARMMAN](https://www.armman.org/mmitra/)) | One-way, pregnancy only. |
| **Maya** (Plackal, Bengaluru) | Period and pregnancy tracker | Claims 100M downloads, mostly outside India; under 1% Indian users in early years ([ShethePeople](https://www.shethepeople.tv/news/googles-rajan-anandan-invests-in-menstrual-health-tracker-maya)) | Criticised for sharing data with Facebook ([Privacy International](https://privacyinternational.org/long-read/5564/maya-research-findings)). Generic, not built around Indian public health. |
| **Flo / Clue** (global) | Period tracking | Large user base in India | English-first, aimed at city users, ads and paid tiers, no Indian schemes or ASHA link. |
| **Veera Health** | Digital PCOS care programme | $3M seed, Sequoia Surge ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=258241)) | Paid, PCOS only, for urban women. |
| **Proactive For Her, Gytree, Pinky Promise** | Teleconsultation and women's health clinics | Pinky Promise: 350,000 women, 40,000 paying ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/herstory/2026/01/india-startup-day-womens-health-startups-rise-growth)) | Paid, for urban women, needs a doctor appointment. |
| **Sirona** | Hygiene products plus tracker | Consumer brand ([Finology](https://insider.finology.in/startups-india/femtech-startups-india)) | Sells products. No health screening. |
| **Laiqa** | Period products and wellness | ₹15 crore seed ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/laiqa-seeks-to-capitalise-on-its-femtech-offerings-with-funding-from-ivycap)) | Product brand. |
| **Government menstrual hygiene schemes** (RKSK and the Tamil Nadu scheme) | Free or cheap pads through ASHA workers and schools | 1.21 crore a year in Tamil Nadu | Supply only. Nothing helps her use them well, track her health or spot problems. |

### Why SAKHI is needed in India

1. **Nothing in the free public system tracks her health.** Kilkari sends one-way calls and the pad schemes only supply products. SAKHI connects the two: her logs, the pad supply, ASHA workers and the doctor.
2. **Paid femtech serves women in cities.** Veera, Pinky Promise and Proactive charge for programmes or consultations. SAKHI is free for women and paid for by institutions (see [BUSINESS.md](BUSINESS.md)).
3. **Tamil language support is missing.** Kilkari's 11 languages don't include Tamil. SAKHI supports Tamil, Telugu, Hindi and English.
4. **Most women don't own a BP machine.** SAKHI accepts readings from her MCP card, her ASHA worker or a health centre, and asks for a fresh check when a reading gets old. Consumer apps assume home devices.
5. **Privacy on shared phones.** Maya was criticised for sharing data. SAKHI has no ads, has discreet mode, and lets her export or delete her data.
6. **Hygiene leads to health.** No competitor connects how she uses pads, through the blood-loss score, to the iron check and a doctor visit.

## 5. Model validation

### PCOS symptom model
I found no second open dataset with the same self-reported questions:
- the IEEE DataPort survey needs an institutional subscription,
- the ICMR PCOS repository doesn't share raw data,
- Flo's 40,000-woman study is not public.

So the model was stress-tested on the Kerala data (541 women) instead, using `scripts/validate_pcos.py`, 10 × 5-fold cross-validation and 1,000 bootstrap samples:

| Situation | ROC-AUC (95% CI) | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every answer known | **0.88** (0.85–0.91) | 0.86 | 0.76 |
| BMI and age unknown (common in the app) | 0.87 (0.84–0.91) | 0.86 | 0.73 |
| Only her answers and cycle regularity | 0.87 (0.83–0.90) | 0.88 | 0.72 |

Sensitivity and specificity are at the 0.23 screening cut-off.

**Calibration is good.** Women given a 75–100% chance had PCOS 88% of the time (the model predicted 88%). Those given under 10% had it 8% of the time (predicted 5%).

**By age and BMI:**
- Under 25: n = 50, AUC 0.98. This is a small group, so treat the result with caution.
- 25 and over: AUC 0.87.
- BMI under 25: AUC 0.85. BMI 25 and over: AUC 0.91.

**Main gap:** only 50 women in the data are under 25, and this is hospital data from Kerala. The next step is a survey of young women in Tamil Nadu, with consent, in the pilot.
